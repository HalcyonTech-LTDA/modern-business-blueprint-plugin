# IP & Legal Asset Protection

This skill provides the legal frameworks, intellectual property defense protocols, and essential commercial contract architecture for the **Corporate Architect Agent** (acting as combined Chief Technology Officer and General Counsel) to secure 100% ownership of company assets, protect trade secrets, and establish frictionless enterprise contracts.

---

## Core Principles

1. **Absolute, Non-Negotiable 100% IP Ownership & Chain of Title**:
   - In accordance with **Rule 3 (Constraint)**, intellectual property ownership is non-negotiable. The operating company must own 100% of all structural assets:
     - Source code repositories and architecture documentation.
     - Decision algorithms, heuristics, and mathematical models.
     - Declarative rules engines and schema configurations.
     - Corporate domains, trademark filings, and DNS custody.
     - Synthetic and curated datasets, embeddings, and context graphs.
   - **Outsourcing Trap Defense**: Early-stage companies frequently hire external software agencies, development shops, or independent contractors to accelerate initial builds. Without rigorous, present assignment agreements, copyright and patent rights legally reside with the developer or agency under default copyright law, creating fatal chain-of-title defects during VC due diligence or acquisition audits.
   - **Remedy**: Every contractor, agency, and employee must execute a comprehensive **Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA)** containing explicit "hereby assigns" language, full moral rights waivers, work-for-hire declarations, and mandatory immediate repository turnover.

2. **Total Custody of Structural Infrastructure Assets**:
   - Software development agencies and contractors must never register domain names, cloud provider accounts (AWS, GCP, Azure), GitHub organizations, or third-party API keys in their personal or agency names.
   - 100% of administrative roots and account ownership must reside exclusively in corporate-owned email addresses (`@company.com`) controlled by the founders.

3. **Essential Early-Stage Contract Suite**:
   - An early-stage enterprise company must maintain a standardized, battle-tested 4-tier contract suite designed to protect enterprise value while streamlining procurement:
     - **Enterprise SaaS Agreement / Master Services Agreement (MSA)**: Governs subscriptions, platform access, intellectual property reservations, mutual liability limitations, and payment terms.
     - **Data Processing Agreement (DPA)**: Establishes statutory data protection compliance (GDPR Art. 28, LGPD, CCPA), controller/processor boundaries, security safeguards, sub-processor notification, and breach response obligations.
     - **Service Level Agreement (SLA)**: Establishes availability uptime commitments (e.g., 99.9%), support response times across severity tiers, and defines service credits as the customer's *sole and exclusive financial remedy*.
     - **Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA)**: Bilateral confidentiality agreement protecting proprietary architectures, benchmarks, pricing models, and trade secrets, strictly rejecting broad "residuals" clauses.

4. **Elimination of Structural Vendor Lock-In & Client Exclusivity**:
   - Contracts with enterprise customers must explicitly state that while customer-provided data remains the property of the customer, the platform, underlying models, generic rules, aggregated metadata, and system improvements remain the sole and exclusive intellectual property of the company.
   - Reject customer demands for IP ownership of features, bespoke code forks, or restrictive commercial exclusivity.

### Step 1: Chain-of-Title Audit & PIIA Execution
1. Audit the historical contributions of all founders, co-founders, early advisors, and employees.
2. Require every contributor to sign a **Proprietary Information and Inventions Agreement (PIIA)** with:
   - Present tense assignment language: *"The Contributor hereby irrevocably assigns, transfers, and conveys to the Company all right, title, and interest in and to all Inventions, Works, and Intellectual Property."*
   - Full waiver of moral rights to the fullest extent permitted by law.
   - Explicit covenant that no pre-existing third-party or open-source copyleft IP has been commingled without prior written approval.

### Step 2: Outsourced Development Agency Covenant Architecture
1. When retaining external software factories, specialized contractors, or dev shops:
   - Insert mandatory **Work-for-Hire** covenants.
   - Enforce staged source code delivery: Code must be committed daily to the company's private Git repository. Final payment milestones are strictly contingent upon clean IP assignment execution and full administrative handover.
   - Prohibit agency retention of proprietary tools or "background technology" within the company's core codebase unless fully licensed on a perpetual, irrevocable, worldwide, royalty-free, transferable basis.

### Step 3: Enterprise SaaS Master Services Agreement (MSA) Design
1. Define the subscription scope: The customer receives a non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable license to access the platform.
2. Draft the **Intellectual Property Reservation**:
   - Customer owns all Customer Input Data.
   - Company owns all platform code, algorithms, workflows, schemas, and aggregated anonymous operational telemetry.
3. Establish liability protections:
   - Cap total aggregate liability to the fees paid by customer in the preceding 12-month period.
   - Mutual waiver of consequential, indirect, special, and punitive damages.
   - Carve-outs limited solely to gross negligence, intentional misconduct, and indemnification for third-party IP infringement.

### Step 4: Statutory Data Processing Agreement (DPA) Alignment
1. Classify legal roles: Customer is the Data Controller; Company is the Data Processor.
2. Define the scope, duration, and categories of personal data processed.
3. Establish security commitments (AES-256, TLS 1.3, SOC 2 alignment).
4. Define the security incident notification window ($\le 48-72$ hours following confirmed unauthorized access).
5. Require customer authorization for sub-processors, with automated email notification and reasonable objection mechanisms.

### Step 5: Service Level Agreement (SLA) & Remedy Defense
1. Commit to standard commercial uptime ($\ge 99.9\%$, representing approximately 43.8 minutes of unplanned downtime per month).
2. Explicitly exclude planned maintenance windows (announced with $\ge 48$ hours notice) and customer network failures from downtime calculations.
3. Classify incident severity (Severity 1 Critical down to Severity 4 Informational) with defined target response times.
4. Mandate that **Service Credits** are the customer's *sole and exclusive financial remedy* for SLA breaches, capped at 15% - 20% of the applicable monthly subscription fee.

### Step 6: Non-Disclosure Agreement (NDA) & Trade Secret Defense
1. Mandate standard bilateral (mutual) non-disclosure terms.
2. Define Confidential Information clearly, covering source code, architecture diagrams, product roadmaps, and commercial terms.
3. Establish a standard survival period of 3 to 5 years, with the critical exception that **Trade Secrets** remain confidential indefinitely for as long as they retain trade secret status under applicable law.
4. **Residuals Clause Ban**: Strictly strike any customer clause permitting the other party to use "intangible residuals retained in the unaided memory of personnel", which could allow unauthorized reconstruction of proprietary algorithms.
