# Defensibility & Derived Data in Venture Capital

In early-stage enterprise software and AI infrastructure, features are ephemeral and foundation models rapidly commoditize standalone wrappers. This reference explains how proprietary derived data and accumulated decision graphs create enduring venture-scale enterprise value.

---

## 1. Raw Data vs. Derived Data Economics

| Dimension | Raw External Data (Commodity) | Proprietary Derived Data (Alpha) |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Source** | Public registries, credit bureaus, commercial vendors (D&B, LexisNexis). | Programmatic operational transactions executed across the company's client base. |
| **Availability** | Available to anyone with a corporate credit card. | Exclusive to the platform; cannot be scraped, simulated, or purchased. |
| **Marginal Cost** | Linear per-query fee ($0.10 - $2.50 per hit) paid to third parties. | Marginal cost approaches zero ($< $0.0001); 95%+ gross margin. |
| **Competitive Moat** | Zero defensibility; creates margin squeeze. | Compounding data network effect; creates widening competitive gulf. |
| **Valuation Impact** | Penalized as a data reseller (1-3x multiple). | Rewarded as proprietary decision intelligence (10-15x multiple). |

## 3. The Four Non-Feature Defensibility Vectors in Venture Underwriting

### Vector 1: Operational Integration Gravity
- When software is integrated into an enterprise's real-time transaction processing path (e.g., ISO 20022 payment streams, Kafka topics), removing it requires an architectural overhaul, internal engineering changes, and multi-departmental approvals.
- Result: **Gross Retention Rates (GRR) exceeding 96%**.

### Vector 2: The Regulatory / Statutory System of Record
- Once a compliance regulator or internal audit committee reviews and approves the system's cryptographic audit logs, replacing the platform triggers renewed supervisory scrutiny and audit exposure.
- Enterprise customers become structurally risk-averse to changing vendors.

### Vector 3: Two-Sided & Data Network Effects
- In consortium networks (e.g., fraud prevention, supply chain verification), every new bank or supplier onboarding adds nodes and edges to the collective risk graph.
- A fraudulent counterparty identified at Bank A is automatically surfaced and mitigated for Bank B in real time, creating an insurmountable network barrier for any single-tenant entrant.

### Vector 4: Accumulated Context & Calibration Graph
- Over 2-3 years of operation, the platform accumulates millions of historical verdicts, customer-specific exception rules, and machine-learned weights tailored to the enterprise's operational risk appetite.
- A competitor starting from scratch would generate an unacceptably high rate of false positives ($> 50\%$), creating immediate operational chaos if swapped in.
