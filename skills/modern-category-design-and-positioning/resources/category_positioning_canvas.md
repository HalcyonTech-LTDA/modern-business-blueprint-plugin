# Category Positioning Canvas & Evaluation Rubric

Use this canvas to audit and validate the positioning of any emerging corporate concept before committing capital or engineering resources.

---

## 1. Category Diagnostic Questionnaire

Every dimension must receive a definitive, defensible executive answer:

1. **Current Incumbent Framing**: What generic 2-to-3 word term does the customer currently use to search for existing solutions? (e.g., "expense software", "chat bot", "CRM").
2. **The Incumbent Fallacy**: What fundamental architectural compromise do all incumbents make that inherently limits their value?
3. **The Inflection Catalyst**: Why was this company impossible to build 36 months ago, and why will it be commoditized if built 36 months from now?
4. **The Ownable Moniker**: Does the new category moniker convey institutional authority and systemic indispensability?
5. **The Value Transform**: Can the entire company's mechanism be expressed as an arrow-delimited flow ending in high-margin `DECISION`?

---

## 2. Institutional Category Scoring Rubric

Evaluate the proposed category across four criteria on a scale of 1 to 5:

| Criterion | Score (1-5) | Validation Test |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **A. Moniker Authority** | | Does the category sound like mission-critical infrastructure rather than a lightweight feature? |
| **B. Anti-Positioning Clarity** | | Does the "IS NOT" definition cleanly eliminate low-margin feature requests and bespoke service traps? |
| **C. Price Inelasticity** | | Does the positioning untether the product from legacy seat-based pricing benchmarks? |
| **D. Executive Ownership** | | Does the problem resonate at the C-suite level (CFO/CRO/CTO) rather than solely with mid-level managers? |

**Scoring Guide**:
- **16 - 20 points**: *Tier-1 Institutional Category*. Proceed to Economic Architecture.
- **11 - 15 points**: *Refinement Required*. Positioning is still leaking into adjacent legacy spaces.
- **< 11 points**: *Fatal Red-Ocean Risk*. The concept is caught in a commodity feature trap. Ruthlessly pivot or reject.
